london's secret sauna history, part 1.

1600s: london's first steam baths were called bagnios. you could sweat, bathe and get a shave.

1700s: bagnios also rented rooms by the night, and got a scandalous reputation. the painting is 'the bagnio' by william hogarth (1743), from his series marriage a-la-mode: a husband has just been stabbed by his wife's lover, who is escaping through the window.

1862: the victorians went wild for turkish baths, a row of hotter and hotter rooms. the hammam on jermyn street in st james's was one of the grandest. this is its cooling room, drawn for the illustrated london news. a bomb destroyed it in the second world war.

1895: nevill's turkish baths opened just off bishopsgate, by liverpool street. this postcard shows one of its cooling rooms.

today: the tiled entrance kiosk is still standing in bishopsgate churchyard, two minutes from liverpool street station.
